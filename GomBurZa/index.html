CHAPTER I — THE BEGINNING

A Nation Under
Spanish Rule

During the nineteenth century, the Philippines was governed by Spain. Colonial authorities and religious orders held considerable influence over society.

Filipino secular priests faced discrimination and struggled for the right to administer parishes, challenging the privileges of powerful religious orders.

"Before a nation could demand freedom, its people had to recognize injustice."

CHAPTER III — A CALL FOR JUSTICE

Their Fight Was
For Equality

The secularization controversy centered on who should administer Philippine parishes. Filipino secular priests sought fair treatment and greater opportunities in church leadership.

CHAPTER IV — THE TURNING POINT

1872

The Cavite Mutiny

On January 20, 1872, an uprising occurred at the Cavite naval arsenal. Spanish colonial authorities connected the incident to a wider alleged conspiracy.

Gómez, Burgos, and Zamora were accused of involvement, although the case against them remains deeply disputed in historical accounts.

CHAPTER VII — THE LEGACY

Their Story
Did Not End.

The martyrdom of GOMBURZA helped inspire a new generation of Filipino reformers and nationalists.

José Rizal honored the three priests by dedicating his novel El Filibusterismo to their memory. Their names remain an enduring symbol of the demand for dignity and justice.

Their sacrifice became part of the awakening of Filipino national consciousness.
